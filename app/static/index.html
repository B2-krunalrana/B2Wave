Help & guide

Everything about B2Wave in one place. Type to filter.

Getting started (5 minutes)
  1. Insert a nano SIM on the back of the board. Gold contacts face the board, and the SIM PIN lock must be off.
  2. Screw the LTE antenna onto the small gold connector marked LTE.
  3. Plug cable 1 into the ESP_UART port (the USB-C nearer the SIMCom chip) and into the PC.
  4. Double-click run.bat. The browser opens B2Wave.
  5. Open Setup, click Detect board, then Install firmware (first time only).
  6. For SMS, calls and voice campaigns, use the USB link (next section).
Cables, ports and the USB_SELECT switch
Port / switchWhat it doesRecommended
ESP_UART (USB-C nearer the SIMCom chip)Programs and tests the ESP32 (Setup page)Cable 1 → PC
4G_USB (USB-C nearer the ESP32 module)Direct link to the 4G module: SMS, calls, audio uploadCable 2 → PC
USB_SELECT switch (between the two ports)"4G" sends the module's USB to the 4G_USB port. "ESP" sends it to the ESP32 internally.Keep on "4G"
Don't move the switch to ESP while using B2Wave. The app would lose the 4G module, and uploads, SMS and calls would stop.
Drivers (Windows)
Device Manager showsInstall
"CP2102 USB to UART Bridge Controller" with Silicon Labs CP210x driver. Afterwards it shows as "Silicon Labs CP210x … (COMx)".
"Mobile AT Interface" / "Mobile Diag Interface" with SIMCom A76xx driver. Extract it, then Device Manager → Update driver → Browse → pick the folder (tick Include subfolders). Afterwards it shows as "SimTech HS-USB AT Port 9011 (COMx)".
SMS
  • Messages → New, type the number and the text, press Enter.
  • Any language works (Gujarati, Hindi, emoji). Unicode SMS hold 70 characters per part, plain English 160. Long texts are split automatically.
  • Incoming SMS appear immediately, with a sound and a desktop notification (turn notifications on in Settings).
  • Everything is saved on this PC in app/data/messages.json. The SIM holds only about 10 messages, so delete old ones from the SIM when it's full.
Calls
  • Phone: dial with the keypad, then press the green button. Incoming calls pop up with Answer and Decline.
  • During a call, use the # keypad to send tones (for bank and IVR menus) and the slider for board speaker volume.
  • Voice audio goes to the board's SPK/MIC pads, not the PC. See "Talk from your PC" below.
  • Jio: calls use VoLTE, and they work on this module. Airtel and Vi also work.
Bulk SMS
  1. Open Bulk SMS. Paste numbers (one per line, optional name after a comma) or Upload CSV with a number column. Other columns become variables.
  2. Write the message. Click a variable chip to insert {name}. Use {name|Customer} as a fallback for blank values. The preview shows the first recipient's text.
  3. Set the gap between messages (3 s or more is safest with operators) and, optionally, an email for the delivery report.
  4. Click Send. Each number shows sent / failed live. Failed numbers are retried once automatically, and "Retry failed" creates a new job for the rest.
  5. Duplicates and invalid numbers are removed before sending. Every message also appears in Messages.
Email reports (Gmail / SMTP)
  1. Settings, then Email (SMTP). Choose Gmail and enter your address.
  2. Gmail needs an App Password: turn on 2-Step Verification, then create one at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and paste the 16 characters.
  3. Click Send test email to confirm it works.
  4. In a campaign or bulk SMS job, fill Email report to. When it finishes, a summary with a CSV attachment is emailed automatically. Use Email report on any finished job to send it again.
AI voice agent (LiveKit)
  1. Create a free project at cloud.livekit.io and copy the URL, API key and secret. Get an OpenAI API key.
  2. AI voice agent page: paste the keys, click Save, then Test LiveKit.
  3. Double-click agent\run_agent.bat and keep the window open. It sets itself up the first time.
  4. Pick a template or write instructions, an opening line, voice and language.
  5. Test with PC mic: talk to the agent from this PC, with a live transcript.
  6. Call a phone number: B2Wave dials, and when the person answers the agent talks to them. The AI voice goes into the call (the caller hears it) through a USB audio adapter connected to the board's headset socket. See Talk on calls > Let the PC or the AI agent talk on calls. B2Wave never sends it to your PC speakers in call mode.
The agent uses LiveKit Agents 1.0.23. Newer releases load native DLLs that Windows Smart App Control blocks on some PCs.
Backup, restore and moving to another PC
  1. Settings, then Backup & restore, then Download backup: one .zip with messages, calls, campaigns, bulk SMS jobs, AI transcripts, audio prompts and settings.
  2. Passwords and API keys are left out unless you tick Include passwords & API keys. Tick it only for a private move to a new PC.
  3. To restore, drop the .zip on Import, check the preview, then choose Merge (adds what's missing, no duplicates) or Replace.
  4. A safety copy of the current data is saved first in app/data/backups/. Import it with Replace to undo.
  5. The connection settings of this PC (COM port, Wi-Fi bridge) are always kept. Every open B2Wave page reloads with the restored data.
Your SIM number
  • B2Wave reads the SIM's own number and shows it at the top of every page, on Home and in the sidebar. Click it to copy it or see details.
  • Some SIMs don't store their number. The chip then says Set SIM number. Enter it once and it's remembered for that SIM card (by its serial).
  • Swap the SIM and B2Wave notices the new card, reads its number and shows a "New SIM detected" message.
Voice campaigns (play audio, capture 1 / 2)
  1. Campaigns → Upload any audio (MP3, WAV, M4A). It's converted to telephone quality and kept on this PC.
  2. It's copied to the 4G module automatically (8 KB chunks, size-checked). The module holds about 20 s of audio at a time, and older prompts are swapped out automatically.
  3. Paste numbers, choose the prompt, click Start campaign. Each answered call hears the prompt.
  4. When someone presses 1, 2…, it appears under Live key presses and in the results table. Download the results as CSV.
  5. Test with your own number first ("Test with one number").
Script tip: "Hello from B2Wave. Press 1 to confirm. Press 2 to cancel." Keep it under 15 seconds.
Talk from your PC (wiring)
  • This module can't stream call audio over USB or Wi-Fi. We tested it (no AT+CPCMREG on firmware A131B01A7672M6).
  • A: a speaker + electret mic on the SPK/MIC pads (~₹100).
  • B: a USB sound card (~₹250) plus the built-in PC audio bridge (recommended).
  • C: an ES8388/WM8960 I2S codec for voice over Wi-Fi (needs a firmware update).
  • Exact pins and diagrams are on the Audio page.
Troubleshooting
ProblemFix
"Board not found" in SetupInstall the CP210x driver. Use a data cable (not charge-only). Check the red VIN LED.
"ESP32 to 4G serial link: no reply"Use the 4G_USB cable (USB mode). It's fully supported, and it's how B2Wave runs today.
"Windows sees the 4G module but has no driver"Install the SIMCom A76xx driver (Drivers section).
Upload failedBoard must be online (green on Home). Keep the switch on 4G. Big files are trimmed to fit, and the PC copy is always kept, so retry with "Module".
SIM: PIN / not inserted / failureRemove the PIN lock in a phone, reseat the SIM, then Setup → Restart 4G module.
Network "searching" / "denied"Attach the antenna. Check the SIM is active and has balance.
Hear ringing but can't talkExpected: audio is on the SPK/MIC pads. See "Talk from your PC".
"Port 8080 busy"Just run run.bat again. A new launch replaces the old one automatically.
Anything elseSetup → step 3 → Diagnostics report, then send the copied text to support.

SetupWhat works today
PC + 4G_USB cable (switch on 4G) Everything: SMS, calls, campaigns, key capture, audio upload. Use this.
Board on a phone charger + Wi-Fi only The board joins B2-ios and reports status and diagnostics. SMS and calls don't work over Wi-Fi yet, because on this board the ESP32 → 4G serial link doesn't answer.
Board alone, no PC Not yet. B2Wave runs on the PC. The board is the modem.

What B2Wave does automatically

  • Cable replugged? Windows may give it a new COM number (COM9 → COM8). B2Wave finds the module again by itself.
  • Cable unplugged? B2Wave shows a red alert, falls back to the Wi-Fi bridge if one is reachable, and reconnects to USB as soon as the cable is back.
  • Module restarted? Settings are re-applied automatically when the module reports it's ready.
  • App restarted? Running run.bat again replaces the old copy, so there's no "port busy" error.

Roadmap to true standalone

  • Move the switch to ESP and let the ESP32-S3 drive the module over USB (USB-host firmware). Then the PC connects to the board only over Wi-Fi. This is the "ESP32-Controlled LTE Mode" the board advertises.
  • Until then, keep the 4G_USB cable connected to the PC.
FAQ
  • Does Jio work? Yes: SMS and VoLTE calls were tested on Jio.
  • Where is my data? Only on this PC, in app/data/: messages, calls, campaigns, audio. Nothing goes to the cloud.
  • Can I use it from my phone? Start with run.bat --lan, then open http://<PC-IP>:8080 on the same Wi-Fi.
  • Wi-Fi mode? The board joins hotspot B2-ios (password 12345678). SMS and calls over Wi-Fi need the ESP32 to 4G serial link, which is still under investigation on this board, so use USB mode for now.

       Other ways to talk speaker, PC, Wi-Fi
          
      
          
      
        
      
          
      Speaker + mic on the board Easiest · ~₹100
      
          
      You talk at the board, like a desk phone. The app still dials, answers and hangs up.
      
          
      
          
      Parts
      
          
      • 1 × small speaker, 8–32 Ω, 0.5–1 W (any old phone or laptop speaker works)
      • 1 × electret condenser mic capsule (2-pin, 6–9 mm)
      • Thin wires, a soldering iron, or a 2-pin header with jumper wires
      
          
      Steps
      
          
      1. Turn the board over. Near the SIM slot there are two pad pairs marked SPK and MIC, each with a +.
        2. 
          
      2. Solder the speaker: red wire to SPK +, black wire to the other SPK pad.
        3. 
          
      3. Solder the mic: the mic leg not connected to its metal case goes to MIC +. The case leg goes to the other MIC pad.
        4. 
          
      4. Make a call. Adjust loudness with the volume slider on the call screen.
      
        
      

        
      
          
      Through this PC (USB sound card) Recommended for AI calls · ~₹250
      
          
      A cheap USB sound card carries call audio to your laptop. The bridge below plays it on your PC speakers and sends your PC mic into the call. Use headphones to avoid echo.
      
          
      
          
      Parts
      
          
      • 1 × USB sound card with separate mic-in and headphone-out (the ₹150–300 "USB 7.1" dongle)
      • 2 × 3.5 mm mono/stereo cables (cut one end) or 3.5 mm plug breakouts
      • 2 × 10 µF electrolytic capacitors, 1 × 4.7 kΩ, 1 × 10 kΩ, 1 × 100 Ω resistor
      
          
      Steps
      
          
      1. Call audio to PC: board SPK + → 10 µF (+ leg to the board) → 4.7 kΩ → sound card MIC-IN tip. Cable sleeve → board GND (header pin 41 or 43). Leave SPK − unconnected. Never connect it to GND.
        2. 
          
      2. PC voice into the call: sound card HEADPHONE tip → 10 µF → 10 kΩ → board MIC +. Also connect 100 Ω from MIC + to GND (this drops the level about 100×). Cable sleeve → GND.
        3. 
          
      3. Plug the sound card into the laptop. In the bridge below, choose: Call audio in = USB sound card mic, PC speaker = your headphones, PC mic = your laptop mic, Into call = USB sound card output. Then click Start bridge.
